Recommendation: Rejection in its current form, with reconsideration after substantial methodological correction and reanalysis.

The clinical question is relevant, but the manuscript currently does not establish that the proposed algorithm is implemented as described, that its theoretical guarantees hold, or that its reported forecasting performance supports the clinical claims.

The authors propose HBML, an online method for selecting among five personalized glucose-forecasting models. Using individual CGM histories without population pretraining is a useful practical objective, and evaluation across two contrasting cohorts is a strength. However, the principal contribution - reliable adaptation among experts - is not convincingly demonstrated. Several concerns below affect the validity of the central findings rather than their presentation.

Additional problems remain:

The theorem uses αₜ = 1/t, whereas the experiments reportedly use 1/t².

The best expert at each individual round can switch O(T) times; sublinear regret against that comparator is not established.

Replacing cumulative loss with a discounted accumulator does not automatically yield a bound on undiscounted cumulative regret.

Updating an expert's parameters does not, by itself, invalidate an expert-advice guarantee against its sequentially issued predictions. The discussion of parameter convergence needs a clearly defined alternative comparator and a derivation connecting parameter error to cumulative prediction loss.

The theoretical section requires reconstruction, including assumptions, proofs, and treatment of delayed feedback.

4  The proposed learning rate schedule is not scale free in the stated sense

For

multiplying every loss by a > 0 multiplies the accumulator by a, so the exponent in the weight update changes by a factor of √a. The resulting expert probabilities therefore change under a simple rescaling of the losses.

This contradicts the usual scale-invariance property of scale-free online learning.

Source: Orabona and Pál. Scale-Free Online Learning

The practical implication is that expressing otherwise identical glucose measurements in different units can change the algorithm's behavior unless additional normalization is applied. The manuscript must describe such normalization or revise the method and terminology. It must also report the actual values of β and ε, their selection procedures, and sensitivity analyses. The repeated unresolved citations to "Scale-Free Hedge" further prevent verification.

5  The evaluation does not establish that forecasts use only available information

The introduction mentions delayed losses, but Algorithm 1 observes a prediction's outcome within the same iteration without explicitly representing forecast origin, target time, or pending forecasts.

This distinction is essential. On a five-minute sampling grid, a five-hour forecast cannot contribute its realized loss to model selection until 60 subsequent sampling intervals have passed. Updating its weights earlier would introduce future information.

The manuscript needs an explicit chronological evaluation protocol specifying forecast stride, initial training period, refitting times, available training labels, delayed weight updates, and whether errors concern the horizon endpoint or an entire predicted trajectory. It must also document normalization, interpolation, missing-data handling, and the meaning of "full history" at each forecast origin.

The current description does not prove that leakage occurred; it fails to rule it out. A reproducible audit of timestamps and data availability is necessary before the performance estimates can be trusted.

Comparing HBML only with its constituent models does not establish that the proposed selection mechanism provides the benefit. Essential comparators include persistence, simple averaging, performance-weighted averaging, a prospectively selected single expert, and a simple recent-loss switching rule. Ablations should isolate sharing, the adaptive learning rate, damping, and the choice of sharing schedule.

8  The statistical analysis is missing and evaluation set tuning is a concern

The "Statistics and reproducibility" section remains a placeholder. Reporting mean participant RMSE with its standard deviation does not quantify uncertainty in the paired difference between methods.

The authors should report participant-level paired improvements with confidence intervals, the proportion of participants benefiting or worsening, and sensitivity to stochastic model training and expert sampling. Resampling should preserve each participant's longitudinal dependence rather than treating overlapping forecasts as independent observations.

The statement that HBML wins 23 of 24 configurations is descriptive; those configurations are correlated and do not constitute 24 independent confirmations. Similarly, "matched" performance is not established by numerical proximity alone.

The manuscript states that 1/t² was selected because it performed as well as or better than 1/t across the evaluated settings. This is a data-driven method choice. The authors must identify development and evaluation data and show that the final pipeline was assessed independently of such choices. The explanation attributing one unfavorable result to initialization also requires an ablation rather than speculation.

9  The clinical safety and early warning claims exceed the evidence

The Discussion claims high sensitivity and specificity, a safety-net property, and suitability for interventions including insulin administration. These claims are not supported by the reported analyses.

Clarke Error Grid proportions provide a descriptive assessment of prediction errors. They do not establish event-detection sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, false alarms per day, warning lead time, or clinical benefit. In particular, the fraction of all predictions in Zone C is not a conventional false-positive rate, and the combined D+E fraction is not a conventional false-negative rate: the denominators and event definitions differ.

High overall A+B percentages can coexist with poor detection of uncommon but important hypoglycemic episodes. The reported 0.93% Zone E at five hours in Weinstock also warrants investigation of affected participants and episodes rather than reassurance from the combined A+B percentage.

The authors should define the intended use - such as short-term hypoglycemia alerts or longer-term glucose trend information - and evaluate the corresponding event-level outcomes. A five-hour endpoint forecast can miss an intervening hypoglycemic event entirely. Forecast-informed treatment also changes future glucose, so retrospective accuracy cannot establish the safety of acting on those forecasts.

Until those evaluations exist, claims of clinical reliability, a guaranteed safety net, and immediate deployment should be substantially narrowed.

10  Figure 4 frequencies may be driven by tie handling

Algorithm 1 returns exactly the prediction of one constituent expert. Therefore, at an individual forecast origin, HBML's squared error must equal that expert's error: HBML cannot uniquely outperform all five experts at that origin.

Nevertheless, Figure 4 presents HBML as the most frequently best-performing method. The authors must explain whether comparisons are pointwise or calculated over windows, how ties are counted, and whether ordering or tie-breaking favors HBML. This is especially important for the A+B criterion, where many methods can receive the same binary classification.

Regime-specific RMSE differences and event-detection performance, with sample sizes and uncertainty, would be more interpretable. The regime definitions should also specify whether they use only past measurements and how stable but out-of-range glucose is classified.

11  Cohort selection and preprocessing are inadequately documented

The original Weinstock study included 201 participants, whereas this manuscript analyzes 100. The manuscript must explain the selection process, exclusions, and representation of participants with and without prior severe hypoglycemia. These details directly affect the claimed relevance to hypoglycemia prevention.

Source: Weinstock et al. Risk Factors Associated With Severe Hypoglycemia in Older Adults With Type 1 Diabetes

CGMacros includes both Libre and Dexcom sensors with different native sampling intervals, and its distributed files use a one-minute time grid. The manuscript does not identify the glucose stream used or explain its temporal processing.

Source: CGMacros dataset documentation

A cohort and data-processing table should report recording duration, usable observations, missingness, sensor type, treatment characteristics where available, exclusions, and glycemic-event counts. The authors should also acknowledge that CGMacros involved structured dietary procedures and that its diabetes subgroups contain only 14-16 participants.

Two independently sourced datasets are useful, but they do not establish independent validation of a locked pipeline if methodological choices were informed by both.

12  The runtime and reproducibility evidence is insufficient for the real time claim

Table 11 reports individual expert runtimes rather than measured end-to-end HBML latency. The framework must generate the forecasts needed for all-expert loss updates, maintain weights, and periodically refit models.

The authors should report hardware, synchronization where relevant, total forecasting latency, tail latency, memory use, and refitting overhead. A mean below one second does not establish that every forecast meets that latency. The reported NODE training time of approximately 0.0105 seconds, alongside a description of 100 training epochs per cycle, particularly requires clarification of the timed operation.

A reproducible release should identify the exact code commit, experiment configurations, seeds, preprocessing scripts, and commands that regenerate the manuscript's outputs. The journal requires availability of custom code central to the conclusions for peer review.
